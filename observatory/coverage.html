SAR satellites

Reading the plot: the station's sky seen from above, north at the top, east to the right, the horizon at the rim and straight up at the centre. Each burst the rollup matched to its satellites is checked against every SAR satellite that could have relayed it: above ° at the station, and high enough above the beacon. Each of those is an expected copy, counted where that satellite was in the station's sky; the copies the station actually decoded are the heard ones. Hear rate is heard over expected. It is never near 100%: a beacon's burst reaches six to eight satellites, and only the strongest copies decode. So read the plot for where the station hears better or worse. Grey cells have fewer than expected copies so far. The dashed line is the effective horizon: in each direction, the lowest cell that hears at least half as well as the station's typical cell above 30°. Trees, roofs and the mounting show as a horizon that rises. Satellites now draws the SAR satellites in the station's sky, with their tracks from an hour ago (dotted) to three hours ahead. Only bursts from beacons that report their own position can be matched to satellites, so the plot fills in over a day or more.
